Welcome!

This course is designed to help you get ready for learning at a distance. You will practise finding your way around an online course, planning your studies, learning with others, and knowing where to ask for help.

You do not need to know everything before you begin. The goal is to build confidence and useful habits one step at a time.

Start here

Before you begin

Set aside time to work through the four units over two weeks. You may complete the activities in shorter study sessions. The suggested schedule is flexible; it is not a set of live attendance sessions.

What you will learn

  1. Navigate an online learning environment and locate important course resources.
  2. Develop a realistic study plan suited to your responsibilities.
  3. Take part in meaningful online interaction using appropriate communication methods.
  4. Identify support services and explain when and how to approach them.

How to use this course

  1. Begin with the Start Here section of each unit.
  2. Read or explore the lesson materials.
  3. Complete the activity and keep your output.
  4. Try the self-check and read the feedback.
  5. Reflect, then use the Next Step Guide.

This is for personal reflection, not a test or a measure of academic ability.

Unit 01 · Finding Your Way

Know where to begin

Your first task is to become familiar with the online space where your learning happens.

Suggested pace: Days 1–2

🧭Start Here

When you enter an online course, it can be difficult to know which page to open first. Begin by looking for the course outline or syllabus, announcements, learning materials, activity instructions, deadlines, and submission areas.

Institutions use different learning management systems (LMS), but the information you need is often similar. Take time to explore before a deadline arrives.

🔎Explore: a simple course-navigation checklist

  • Course overview: What is the course about, and what are its learning outcomes?
  • Announcements: Where does the instructor post updates?
  • Learning materials: Where can you find readings, videos, and other resources?
  • Activities and deadlines: Where are instructions and due dates posted?
  • Submission area: How do you submit work and check whether it was received?
  • Communication: Where can you contact the instructor or classmates?

Unit 02 · Learning Independently

Make a plan you can follow

Independent learning involves making decisions about your study, while recognizing when you need guidance or support.

Suggested pace: Days 3–5

🧭Start Here

Flexible learning gives you some control over when and where you study. That flexibility also means you need to plan how learning will fit into your week.

🔎Explore: build a realistic routine

  • Check the requirements: List readings, activities, and deadlines.
  • Estimate the time: Break large tasks into smaller sessions.
  • Choose study periods: Consider work, family responsibilities, rest, and other commitments.
  • Plan for interruptions: Leave some room to adjust when a week does not go as expected.
  • Review your plan: Notice what worked and what needs to change.

Use active reading strategies

  • Preview: Look at the title, headings, learning outcomes, and questions before reading.
  • Read with a purpose: Keep the activity question or learning outcome in mind.
  • Pause and process: After a section, summarize its main idea in your own words.
  • Record questions: Note unfamiliar terms or points you may need to clarify.

Manage distractions

  • Choose a study place or time when interruptions are less likely.
  • Silence non-essential notifications when possible.
  • Work in short, focused periods and take breaks.
  • If interruptions happen, identify the next small task rather than abandoning the plan.

A useful plan is not necessarily a perfect timetable. It is a guide that you can realistically follow and revise.

✍️Try It: Help a learner plan their week

Scenario: A learner works on weekdays and helps care for a family member in the evening. This week, the learner has a 20-page reading, a short quiz based on that reading, and a written activity due on Sunday. The learner has a few shorter periods available during the week and one longer period on Saturday.

Help the learner divide the work into manageable study periods instead of leaving everything until the deadline. There is no single perfect schedule. Choose an arrangement that gives the learner time to read, prepare for the quiz, and work on the written activity.

Unit 03 · Learning With Others

Make online interaction meaningful

Online interaction can support learning, but meaningful participation takes more than simply posting a message.

Suggested pace: Days 6–7

🧭Start Here

In distance education, classmates and instructors may not meet in the same physical space. Communication can happen through discussion forums, messages, email, audio, video, or live sessions.

🔎Explore: what makes an exchange useful?

  • Be clear: Explain your idea or question so others can understand it.
  • Be considerate: Respond to the message, not by attacking the person.
  • Build on ideas: Ask a follow-up question, give a reason, or offer an example.
  • Choose a suitable mode: Use the communication method that fits the purpose and your access.
  • Make space for others: Invite participation without assuming everyone is comfortable speaking in the same way.

A discussion forum creates an opportunity to interact; it does not automatically create connection. The quality and purpose of the exchange matter.

✍️Try It: Introduce yourself and exchange ideas

First, prepare a short introduction for a learning group. You may use text, audio, or video depending on your access and comfort. Include only information you are comfortable sharing, such as what you hope to learn and one thing that may help you participate.

Next, read this example from a classmate: “I find it difficult to keep up with readings because I have other responsibilities.” Write a response that acknowledges the concern and adds something useful. You might ask a respectful question, share a practical suggestion, or point the learner toward a relevant course resource.

Unit 04 · Knowing When and Where to Ask

Find the right kind of support

Being an independent learner includes recognizing when to seek help and knowing where to turn.

Suggested pace: Days 8–9

🧭Start Here

Different concerns may need different kinds of support. A technical problem may require help with an account or platform. A question about an assignment may need an instructor or academic adviser. An administrative concern may need the office responsible for student records or registration.

🔎Explore: match each concern to the right support pathway

Different concerns may need help from different people or offices. Read each situation and choose the kind of support you would approach first.

Situation 1

You understand the topic generally, but you are unsure how to interpret the instructions for an assignment.

Situation 2

You cannot log in to your learning platform, even after checking that you entered your username and password correctly.

Situation 3

You need to correct an error in your student record or ask about your registration status.

Academic support

Questions about course content, learning tasks, feedback, or study strategies.

Technical support

Problems logging in, accessing materials, or using the learning platform.

Administrative support

Questions about registration, records, procedures, or other student services.

Before sending a request, explain what you were trying to do, what happened, and what help you need. Do not include passwords or other sensitive information.
